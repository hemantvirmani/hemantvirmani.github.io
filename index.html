who I am

I’m an engineering leader and a builder. At Adobe, I moved from hands-on engineering into leadership. At Amazon, I led engineering and science organizations across cloud systems, identity, logistics, computer vision, and AI.

Today, I advise product and technology leaders on product strategy, GenAI adoption, cloud strategy, and organizational design. Alongside that work, I continue to prototype agentic AI ideas and write about engineering leadership and AI-native systems.

I am open to the right full-time engineering leadership role, particularly one where I can build and scale products, teams, and organizations. I also take on selected advisory engagements through Xeravis.

LinkedIn has the chronology. This page is the rest: what I like to build, how I think, and what keeps me curious.

I like building things

I'm drawn to problems that initially seem hard to build.

At Adobe, that included Multiple Undo in FrameMaker, a long-awaited feature many thought impractical, and later LeanPrint, technology we patented to reduce paper and ink consumption across Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, the web, and more.

At Amazon, the problems got bigger: Mercury for real-time transportation monitoring; Cairo for document understanding (work that later became AWS Textract's AnalyzeID API); In Person Verification for seller identity; and Deep Thought for Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) classification.

Different problems. Same attraction: ambiguity, difficult engineering, and making something real.

what I believe about engineering
  • Build for outcomes, not output.
  • Understand the system beneath the abstraction.
  • Stay close enough to the technology to have judgment.
  • When something works, make sure it still works at scale.
  • Processes are meant to help people do better work. If they stop helping, scrap them or change them.
  • Smaller teams move faster with fewer meetings and clearer decisions.

why two master's degrees?

I learned computers on the side during my bachelor's and always felt the need to fill the gaps in my understanding.

My first master's, completed part-time through PTU, gave me a stronger foundation but did not satisfy my curiosity. I pursued the second online through BITS Pilani because I wanted to understand the systems beneath the software. I completed both while working full-time. They taught me a great deal, including that curiosity is never fully satisfied.
